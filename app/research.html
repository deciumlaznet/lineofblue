Research and design

The evidence behind Line of Blue

Line of Blue helps police communicators put the verified facts out first, before a false story takes hold. Every screen, workflow and safeguard in it traces back to published law, national guidance, inspection findings, and what the best national teams abroad have learned the hard way.

How it was designed

Requirements engineered from the people who will use it

Line of Blue was specified by working outward from the people involved in a live incident: what each of them needs to know, decide and sign off, and how fast. Each stage fed the next.

STAGE 1

Research

UK policing, international models and the build landscape, each in its own report.

STAGE 2

Actors

Force comms teams, NPCC, Home Office, PCC offices, partners and hostile actors, all fictional.

20 personas · 4 forces
STAGE 3

Scenarios

Incidents that test the hard moments: contempt, false identity, spoof notices, getting ahead.

7 scenarios
STAGE 4

Workflows

Seven workflows on the RESIST 3 stages, each step given an agreed autonomy level.

28 steps · 5 levels
STAGE 5

Actor testing

Every persona walks every scenario on desktop and mobile, with screens judged by eye.

320 test steps
STAGE 6

Governance

A decision log, role limits and a published mandate, so the work can be audited.

Every action logged

Who does the work

Agents take the volume work. People keep every decision that is public, legal or about people. Whatever the level, the agent never publishes, contacts a third party or profiles a person on its own.
